What New York landlords should have ready before their next insurance renewal

A practical checklist for owners, investors, and property managers, whether you have one building or a portfolio.

Candor Insurance Brokerage Inc  |  6 minute read

For many landlords, insurance renewal gets attention only when something goes wrong. A renewal notice arrives with a higher premium. A carrier asks for information nobody has on hand. A lender wants an updated certificate by Friday. Sometimes the notice is a nonrenewal.

Owners who handle renewals well usually aren't doing anything complicated. They have their information organized before the conversation starts. When a licensed producer can see a complete, accurate picture of your properties, the review moves faster and fewer questions come back from underwriters. You also get a clearer conversation about your options.

Start 60 to 90 days before your renewal date

Waiting until the last two weeks leaves little room to review options, answer carrier questions, or deal with a surprise. A two- to three-month window gives your producer time to review your current program and go to market if needed. It also leaves time to resolve issues before your coverage dates arrive.

Common issues worth raising with your producer

Several situations come up often in landlord portfolios and are worth discussing directly with a licensed professional:

  • Properties added mid-year that were never reported to the carrier
  • Title held in an LLC name that doesn't match the policy
  • Units that have been vacant for an extended period
  • Mixed-use buildings with a commercial tenant on the ground floor
  • Short-term, mid-term, or furnished rental arrangements
  • Separate policies on each property that could be reviewed as one portfolio

None of these is unusual. Each is easier to address before renewal than after a claim.
